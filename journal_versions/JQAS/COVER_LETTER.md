# Cover letter — Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports

**Article type:** Research Article  
**Title:** When Should Team KPIs Be Absolute or Relative for Match-Outcome Prediction?  
**Corresponding author:** Rowan Brown, Swansea University, m.r.brown@swansea.ac.uk

Dear Professor Fellingham and Editors,

Please consider this research article for *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports*.

Team key performance indicators in rugby union and association football can be entered into match-outcome models as absolute counts or as differences from the opponent. Relativisation sometimes helps and sometimes harms prediction. Prior rugby studies classified that choice as help versus no help. They did not identify a regime in which the relative feature is the worse classifier.

We organise the choice with a paired efficiency factor that depends on the variance ratio and the within-match correlation, together with signal strength. Across 86 action KPIs from the United Rugby Championship and the English Championship (seasons 23/24–24/25), the inventory is Q3-heavy: 55 of 86 KPIs sit in the anti-correlated regime where absolute features are preferred. Four confirmatory exemplars at matched signal recover both signs under team-blocked cross-validation. We do not claim a global mapping from efficiency to machine-learning gain (pooled $r=0.040$).

The paper is written as a practitioner diagnostic for feature construction, in the same spirit as recent JQAS work on the difficulty of estimating win probability (Brill, Yurko and Wyner, 2025) and on careful football-feature design (Baron et al., 2024; Guan, Sarkar and Swartz, 2024). It also formalises the “relative versus isolated KPI” contrast already shown empirically in the United Rugby Championship (Scott et al., 2023).

The manuscript is original, not under review elsewhere, and approved by all authors. Sports data were analysed under agreement with the United Rugby Championship and Swansea City AFC and are not redistributed. Estimators and the MATLAB pipeline are documented for reproducibility. The review PDF is de-identified; author details are on a separate title page.
