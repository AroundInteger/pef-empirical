\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The cell does not decide the feature}

The four cells record efficiency and variance asymmetry. At matched class separation the exemplar intervals all include zero (\cref{tab:exemplars}), and no Quadrant~3 interval lies entirely below zero. The studies that motivated this work framed the choice as a gain against the absolute feature, or as a null \parencite{bennett2019descriptive,bennett2021predicting,mosey2020,scott2023urc,scott2023womens}. Some KPIs have a negative point estimate of $\Delta\mathrm{ML}$. On these intervals that sign is not separated from fold noise.

Quadrant membership remains a coarse description. The PEF surface is nonlinear in $(\kappa,\rho)$ and in the relative signal, so two KPIs in the same cell need not share the same $\Delta\mathrm{ML}$.

The analysis is univariate by design (\cref{sec:ml_cv}). The motivating studies used multi-KPI classifiers, where inter-feature correlations lie outside a single paired feature. This paper reports the univariate comparison. It does not treat that comparison as a bound on a multivariate effect that was not estimated. The four exemplars illustrate the pre-specified rule at typical class separation. The estimate of whether $\eta$ tracks $\Delta\mathrm{ML}$ is the full cloud (Supplementary~\cref{fig:si_ipred_vs_dml}).

\subsection{Efficiency versus Information}

$\eta$ and $I(X;Y)$ answer different questions. They meet in \cref{eq:mi_closed} only through $d_{\mathrm{rel}}$. At a fixed ratio $\delta/\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}$, a larger $\eta$ raises $d_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and lowers exact $I(X;Y)$. Classical advice to avoid the difference when $\eta<1$ is a variance rule. It is not a prediction rule. Inside the probit model the accuracy gain depends on $(d_{\mathrm{rel}},r_{AX})$ and is bounded below by zero, including at the Fisher point $\eta=1$.

The PEF formula (\cref{eq:pef}) requires only finite second moments. The information-content relationship requires bivariate normality (A1). The efficiency characterisation is therefore the more general result.

\subsection{Practical Guidance}
\label{sec:practical_guidance}

A practical reading of the estimates is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Estimate $\kappa$ and $\rho$ from available data.
  \item Compute $\eta$ using \cref{eq:pef}.
  \item Read the cell from \cref{tab:quadrants} as an efficiency and asymmetry label.
  \item Do not treat the cell as a decision to relativise. Compare the absolute feature, the difference, and, where it is feasible, $(X_{\mathrm{A}},X_{\mathrm{B}})$, and report an interval for $\Delta\mathrm{ML}$.
  \item Inside the probit model, $\Delta\mathrm{ML}\ge 0$. A negative empirical point estimate is outside that model and, on the Quadrant~3 intervals here, is consistent with fold noise.
\end{enumerate}
Possession-normalised rates are a different construction from opponent differences \parencite{sever2026}. They adjust for game state rather than pairing two teams' counts. An open implementation is provided (Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_practitioner}; \cref{sec:data_availability}).

\subsection{The Same Geometry Outside Sport}

The third question is whether the same geometry appears outside sport. Market-adjusted returns, paired clinical designs, and relative sports metrics are instances of correlation-based variance reduction with different $(\kappa,\rho)$ values. A further analogue is value-added benchmarking in education, which compares teacher performance with that of comparable cohorts \parencite{hanushek2010}.

Healthcare pairing sits in Quadrant~2 (\cref{tab:validation}): $\rho$ is high and $\eta>1$, so pairing is efficient.
That cell records efficiency and which margin is more variable.
It does not recommend a feature.
Invasion-game KPIs occupy all four cells and concentrate in Quadrant~3 (\cref{sec:results}).
Whether the difference changes accuracy is a comparison of classifiers, reported with an interval.
The cell does not settle that comparison.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Distributional assumptions.} The PEF formula is distribution-free, but the information content relationship (\cref{eq:mi_closed}) assumes bivariate normality. Paired differences are often closer to normal than the raw series (Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_normality}). Strongly skewed or multimodal distributions may still need a non-parametric mapping. For count-based KPIs, $Z=\log(X+1)$ can improve normality whilst preserving the $(\kappa,\rho)$ structure. Across \PEFrugbyNkpis{} rugby KPIs the rank ordering by $\rho$ was largely preserved (Spearman $r_{s}=0.967$), and the mean of the KPI-wise ratios $\eta_{\log}/\eta$ was $1.085$ (paired $t$, $p=0.357$; Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_qc}). Individual count-based KPIs can still move substantially (rucks won).

\textbf{Stationarity.} The framework treats $\kappa$ and $\rho$ as fixed. Among the rugby and football KPIs, most drift modestly between seasons 23/24 and 24/25, though a minority of rugby KPIs cross quadrant boundaries (\cref{fig:pef_landscape}). Pooled two-season estimates (as in \cref{tab:exemplars}) therefore summarise a moving target.

\textbf{Binary outcomes.} Both \cref{eq:mi_closed} and the sports protocol target binary classification: which team wins a head-to-head match. That choice matches the probit link under (A1)--(A2) and keeps the KPI comparison on a common scale. Multi-class outcomes and continuous targets would need a corresponding information-theoretic mapping. The per-KPI $(\kappa,\rho)$ diagnostic still applies wherever paired absolute and relative features can be formed.

\textbf{Strategic interactions.} In invasion-game sport, teams respond to opponents' tactics and game state. The $(\kappa,\rho)$ structure of a KPI is therefore partly an equilibrium property of competition, not a fixed physical law. Parameters here are estimated from observed match data without a model of within-game adaptation. Where such dynamics are material, $(\kappa,\rho)$ should be read as descriptive summaries at the chosen time scale.

\textbf{Primary evidence base.} The rugby and football KPIs, the machine-learning validation, and the quadrant exemplars are the primary evidence. Healthcare, clinical genomics, and finance show that the same $(\kappa,\rho)$ geometry recurs (\cref{tab:validation}), but without the same per-unit ML scrutiny or temporal replication.

\subsection{Future Directions}

A distribution-free analogue of $I(X;Y)$, for example a rank-based or permutation measure, would extend the integral beyond bivariate normality (Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_qc}). Multivariate classifiers are a different question from the univariate comparison reported here.

Rolling-window or state-space estimates of $(\kappa,\rho)$ would track parameter drift. Season-blocked or chronological cross-validation would tighten temporal leakage beyond the home-team folds used here (\cref{sec:ml_cv}).

Relativisation is often justified as baseline cancellation rather than variance reduction. Connecting the geometry to causal estimands would show when differencing preserves a treatment or exposure contrast and when it introduces collider or selection bias.
