# Sports paper: questions and flags

This branch starts after the `gandalf-grey` tag and after the numerics commit. The tag is the manuscript in which pairing efficiency does not choose the feature. Do not rewrite by restoring the probit story, \(d_{\mathrm{rel}}\), or a quadrant that chooses the column.

Phase 2 responds to white review 1. The draft stays lean and self-contained. Essential alignment and \(1/(2n)\) claims live in a short classical SI. The companion keeps geometry, stress, and Proposition D.

## Questions

1. Are these United Rugby Championship and Championship KPIs aligned with home win?
2. How large is the team-blocked gap of \(D\) relative to \((A, B)\) and to \(A\)?
3. Does \(\eta\), or the quadrant, sort that gap?

Answer those questions with an intercept-only skill gate. A median KPI near the base-rate entropy does not show that informative KPIs are aligned.

## What may be shown

\(D = A - B\), the null \(\beta_A + \beta_B = 0\), and \(1/(2n_{\mathrm{train}})\) from leave-one-team-out training sizes. The formula for \(\eta\) once, as the object being refused as a switch. Algebraic \(r_A=\mathrm{Corr}(A,D)\) as a second sorter beside \(\eta\). Intercept-only log-loss, per-KPI skill, and an informative subset (skill bootstrap interval excludes zero). The sport-level likelihood-ratio distribution, with landmarks at \(2\) (Akaike / CV crossover) and \(3.84\) (\(\chi^2_1\) 5% point). The median team-blocked gaps in milli-nats, with bootstrap intervals, for all KPIs and for the informative subset. The four pre-specified exemplars. A quadrant count with the statement that the count does not choose the feature.

SI may include a short classical derivation of alignment as \(\beta_A+\beta_B=0\) and of the \(1/(2n)\) cost (Akaike, Stone, White). Geometry, \(\psi\), the partition function, and the stress grid stay in the companion.

## Flags that fail this file

- A derivation of \(\cos^2\theta\), of the general form of Proposition D, of \(\psi\), of the partition function, or of the stress grid in the main text.
- Restoring the probit model, \(d_{\mathrm{rel}}\), or \(I(X;Y)\) surfaces.
- \(\eta\), a quadrant, \(\kappa\), or \(\rho\) as the subject of a sentence that chooses the feature. The passing verbs are describes, and does not choose.
- Rugby or football called Quadrant 4, or mostly Quadrant 4. The landscape file is Quadrant-3-heavy.
- A KPI called confirmatory that is not one of `lineout_throws_lost`, `rucks_won`, `corners`, `fouls`.
- A quoted correlation of \(\eta\) with an accuracy change above 0.2, or the words strongly correlated next to \(\eta\) and the gain.
- \(\eta_S/\eta_N\) quoted for a KPI whose `quote_split` entry is false.
- A \(p\)-value or the word significant on one KPI's likelihood ratio.
- Leave-one-team-out described as excluding both clubs from training. Only the held-out team is removed; the opponent's other matches remain.
- Football seasons filtered with the string `23/24`.
- \(1 - h(\Phi(d_{\mathrm{rel}}/2))\) reported as mutual information.
- `eq:dml_poly`, Bosch, manufacturing, or C-MAPSS.
- A hand-written value that belongs in generated `sports_numbers.tex`.
- The conclusion saying both that \(\kappa\) and \(\rho\) decide the feature and that the cell does not.
- The home–away mean gap plugged into \(I(X;Y)\).
- Finance placed in Quadrant 2. With the index as \(A\) and the stock as \(B\), the typical cell is Quadrant 1.
- A multivariable all-KPI ridge model treated as a result of this phase.
- Essential claims that rest only on the unpublished companion.
