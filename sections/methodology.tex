\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study design}

The primary units are team KPIs from professional rugby union and association football.
For each KPI the home series is $A$, the away series is $B$, and the difference is $D=A-B$.
The outcome is home win versus not.
Draws are coded as not a home win.
Across the pooled fixtures the home-win rates are $\PEFrugbyHomeWin\%$ in rugby and $\PEFfootHomeWin\%$ in football.
The analysis reports a likelihood-ratio statistic for $\beta_A+\beta_B=0$, leave-one-team-out log-loss for an intercept-only model and for $A$, $D$, and $(A,B)$, and the second-moment quantities $\eta$ and $r_A$.

\subsection{Primary data}

Rugby KPIs are from the United Rugby Championship over seasons 23/24--24/25 ($\PEFrugbyNgames$ matches; $\PEFrugbyNkpis$ KPIs).
Football KPIs are from the English Championship over the same two seasons ($\PEFfootNgames$ matches; $\PEFfootNkpis$ KPIs).
Performance-indicator construction in invasion games is well established \parencite{hughes2002,bornn2021}.
Rugby records were supplied through access arranged with Scarlets RFC.
Football records were analysed under agreement with Swansea City AFC.
Neither source is redistributed (\cref{sec:data_availability}).

\subsection{Parameter estimation}

For each KPI, $\hat\kappa=\hat\sigma_B^2/\hat\sigma_A^2$ and $\hat\rho$ are the sample variance ratio and Pearson correlation of the home and away series across fixtures.
Then $\hat\eta$ follows \cref{eq:pef} and $\hat r_A$ follows \cref{eq:rA}.
The four cells of $(\kappa,\rho)$ are labelled Quadrants~1--4 in the usual way: Q1 is $\kappa>1$, $\rho>0$; Q2 is $\kappa<1$, $\rho>0$; Q3 is $\kappa<1$, $\rho<0$; Q4 is $\kappa>1$, $\rho<0$.
Uncertainty for $(\hat\kappa,\hat\rho,\hat\eta)$ uses a team-cluster bootstrap on the home-team index ($B=1{,}000$), reported in Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_qc}.

\subsection{Likelihood-ratio statistic}

On the full sample, logistic regression is fitted for $D$ and for $(A,B)$ by maximum likelihood.
Features are centred and scaled by the pooled standard deviation of $A$ and $B$ so that the null $\beta_A+\beta_B=0$ is unchanged by the common scale.
The statistic is \cref{eq:lr}.
Sport-level medians and quartiles summarise the distribution across KPIs.
No per-KPI $p$-value is reported.

\subsection{Team-blocked log-loss and skill}
\label{sec:ml_cv}

Out-of-sample log-loss uses leave-one-team-out scoring.
For each team $t$, the test set is the matches in which $t$ is home or away.
The training set is the remaining matches: only $t$ is held out, so the opponent's other fixtures remain in training.
Each match is scored once for each participating team that yields a valid fold, so a match typically contributes twice to the mean.
Matches with fewer than 20 training rows, fewer than 5 test rows, or a single outcome class in training are skipped.
Mean test log-loss is reported for an intercept-only model and for $A$, $D$, and $(A,B)$.
Skill is $\mathrm{LL}(\mathrm{intercept})-\mathrm{LL}(\mathrm{best\ of\ }A,D,(A,B))$.
A KPI is called informative when the team-bootstrap interval for skill excludes zero.
Gaps are $\mathrm{LL}(D)-\mathrm{LL}(A,B)$ and $\mathrm{LL}(A)-\mathrm{LL}(D)$, converted to milli-nats in the Results.
Negative $\mathrm{LL}(D)-\mathrm{LL}(A,B)$ means $D$ has the lower loss.
The prediction $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{LL}(D)-\mathrm{LL}(A,B)]\approx -1/(2n_{\mathrm{train}})$ uses the mean training-set size across folds.
Uncertainty for per-KPI gaps and skill uses a team multinomial bootstrap with out-of-bag matches (Supplementary~\cref{sec:si_qc}).
Sport-level intervals for median gaps resample KPIs within sport.

\subsection{Pre-specified exemplars}

Four KPIs were selected before the log-loss analysis, after an earlier accuracy-based analysis of the same data: lineout throws lost (rugby, Quadrant~1), rucks won (rugby, Quadrant~2), corners (football, Quadrant~3), and fouls (football, Quadrant~4).
They illustrate one cell each.
They are not confirmatory of a sign for the gap.
