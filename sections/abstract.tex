\begin{abstract}
In rugby union and association football, replacing a team's key performance indicator (KPI) with the match-level difference from its opponent---relativising the feature---sometimes improves match-outcome prediction and sometimes does not. \emph{Why does relativisation help for some KPIs but not others?} Fisher's paired-efficiency formula, $1/(1-\rho)$, explains variance reduction when variances are equal, but head-to-head sport routinely violates that assumption: competitors differ in variability and anti-correlate on high-volume counts, so differencing can \emph{increase} variance whilst still raising predictive power.

We introduce the Paired Efficiency Factor (PEF), $\eta = (1+\kappa)/(1+\kappa-2\sqrt{\kappa}\,\rho)$, a distribution-free efficiency ratio parameterised by the variance ratio $\kappa$ and within-pair correlation $\rho$. Under bivariate normality (A1)--(A2), $\eta$ links to the mutual information between a relative feature and a binary outcome, separating statistical efficiency from information content: relative features can predict better even when $\eta<1$. The $(\kappa,\rho)$ plane yields a four-quadrant taxonomy with distinct feature-engineering guidance.

On United Rugby Championship ($n=\PEFrugbyNgames$ games) and English Championship ($n=\PEFfootNgames$ games) KPIs, the inventory is Q3-dominant; mechanistic confirmation uses four single-KPI exemplars---one per quadrant---at comparable signal strength $\delta/\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}$, showing that the taxonomy predicts the direction of five-fold cross-validated logistic gain. Supporting checks in healthcare, genomics, finance, and manufacturing align with the same geometry. The PEF offers a distribution-free efficiency diagnostic and, under (A1)--(A2), a structured first-pass guide for when to relativise team KPIs in head-to-head prediction.
\end{abstract}
