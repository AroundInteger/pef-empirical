\begin{abstract}
In the United Rugby Championship and the English Championship, a team key performance indicator can enter a home-win model as the home count $A$, the difference $D=A-B$, or the pair $(A,B)$.
This paper asks whether those KPIs are aligned with home win under $\beta_A+\beta_B=0$, how large the team-blocked log-loss gap of $D$ is relative to $(A,B)$ and to $A$, and whether the Paired Efficiency Factor $\eta=(1+\kappa)/(1+\kappa-2\sqrt{\kappa}\,\rho)$ sorts that gap.

Most KPIs sit near the home-win base rate: only $\PEFrugbyNinfo$ of $\PEFrugbyNkpis$ rugby KPIs and $\PEFfootNinfo$ of $\PEFfootNkpis$ football KPIs have positive leave-one-team-out skill with a bootstrap interval excluding zero.
Across all KPIs the median likelihood-ratio statistic for $\beta_A+\beta_B=0$ is $\PEFrugbyMedLR$ in rugby and $\PEFfootMedLR$ in football, both below the feature-choice landmark $\PEFLRaic$.
The median team-blocked gap of $D$ versus $(A,B)$ is $\PEFrugbyMedGapDf$ milli-nats in rugby and $\PEFfootMedGapDf$ in football, matching the prediction $-1/(2n_{\mathrm{train}})$.
Within sport, $\eta$ does not sort that gap; algebraic $r_A=\Corr(A,D)$ is reported as a second candidate for the $A$ versus $D$ comparison.
For a single KPI, the difference loses little to the pair and saves a coefficient; whether it beats the home count alone depends on the KPI, not on pairing efficiency alone.
\end{abstract}
